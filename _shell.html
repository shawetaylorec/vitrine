Background

Orientation

Turns the picture itself, so the width and height swap with it. For hanging something at an angle, use the tilt control on step 3 instead.

Measurement box

Snapping back re-reads it from the cut-out. Cropping throws away everything outside it for good — which is the tidy way to lose a speck you keep missing.

Real size

Derived

Depth cannot be read from a flat photo — type it in. It drives the plan view. For a book, this is the thickness of the closed or open block.

Label

Mounting

Support

Only if the foot is smaller than the object above it — a bowl on a stem, a bust on a socle. Leave them empty and the whole footprint counts. What you put here is what has to fit the plinth; the rest may overhang without complaint.

Fixed to a surface

Stuck flat to the back wall, or to the front of a plinth — no wires, nothing underneath. Either way it faces you in elevation and is barely a line in plan.

Wires

Click the picture where each wire attaches. One point hangs it straight; two points let you tilt it by giving the wires different lengths.

Stand or cradle

Seen from above

Automatic draws a rectangle, or the picture itself once something leans past halfway to flat — which is what you want for a manuscript in a cradle.
